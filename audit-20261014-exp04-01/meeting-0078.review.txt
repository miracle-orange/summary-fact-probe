PILOT SMALL FACTS
1. A call for review process by the City Council requires two council members to initiate an appeal and state a reason for the appeal.
2. The current process allows a single council member to initiate an appeal without stating a reason, which can lead to unnecessary costs and delays for applicants.

PILOT LARGE FACTS
1. Council Member Ashcroft proposed changing the City Council's call for review process to require two council members instead of one to initiate a call for review and to provide a stated reason for the appeal.
2. Currently, a single council member can request a call for review of a board or commission decision without needing to provide a reason.
3. The financial burden of preparing for a call for review falls entirely on the applicant, who must pay a deposit ranging from $750 to $2200, while council members incur no such cost.
4. In one recent example, a homeowner spent $28,000 preparing for a Planning Board appeal and an additional $4,500 for the City Council appeal, which ultimately upheld the Planning Board’s decision.
5. Businesses and residents argue that the current system imposes unnecessary delays and costs, deterring investment and development in Alameda.
6. Supporters of the proposal argue that requiring two council members to initiate a call for review would encourage more thoughtful consideration and reduce frivolous appeals.
7. Opponents argue that limiting access to the call for review process undermines democratic accountability and transparency, as only elected officials (not appointed boards) are known to the public.
8. There is concern among some speakers that the current process allows council members to make calls for review without considering the full implications for staff resources or project timelines.
9. Since 2015, there have been relatively few calls for review compared to the number of decisions made by the Planning Board, with only one call for review in 2017 at the time of the conversation.
10. If passed, the proposal would necessitate changes to the municipal code, potentially taking 6 to 10 months to implement.
11. Some council members expressed support for increasing the threshold to three council members initiating a call for review but accepted the proposal for two as a reasonable alternative.
12. One council member cited past instances where calls for review led to positive outcomes, including modifications to projects or rejection of unsuitable developments.
13. A motion was made and seconded to revise the process, and it carried with a vote of 3 to 2.

REFERENCE FACTS (NOT EXHAUSTIVE GROUND TRUTH)
1. Council Member Ashcraft introduced a referral asking the City Council to revise the process for appealing board and commission decisions.
2. The current City Council policy allows a single Council member to initiate a call for review of a board or commission decision without stating a reason.
3. Council Member Ashcraft's proposal requires that two City Council members initiate a call for review and state a reason for the appeal.
4. Members of the public must pay a monetary deposit to appeal a decision, whereas City Council members are not subject to this financial requirement.
5. Council Member Ashcraft argued that requiring two Council members to initiate a review validates the necessity of the appeal.
6. Council Member Ashcraft cited a recent Planning Board decision where property owners spent an additional $4,500 to prepare for a Council appeal initiated by a single member.
7. Kerry Thompson, representing the Chamber of Commerce, supported the referral, arguing that the current process places an unfair financial burden on businesses and delays projects.
8. Thomas Ellerby, a property owner, supported the referral, stating that a recent call for review on his St. Charles Street property caused financial cost and mental anguish.
9. Janet Gibson opposed the referral, citing a historical example where the call for review process saved a Victorian home on Morton Street.
10. Gretchen Lipow opposed the referral, arguing that requiring two Council members to initiate a review restricts citizen access to the appeal process.
11. Gretchen Lipow presented data indicating that seven out of eight calls for review between 2015 and the current date involved items of high public interest.
12. Michael McDonough, President of the Alameda Chamber of Commerce, supported the referral, noting that the business community has spent millions due to the current review process.
13. Council Member Oddie supported the referral, arguing that Council members should have 'skin in the game' or consensus before initiating a review.
14. Council Member Matarrese supported the requirement for a stated justification but opposed requiring two Council members to initiate a review.
15. Vice Mayor Vella supported the referral, stating that obtaining support from a second Council member is not an overly cumbersome requirement.
16. Mayor Spencer opposed the referral, arguing that the Brown Act restricts Council members to speaking with only one other member outside of meetings.
17. Mayor Spencer argued that the City Council is the elected body responsible for exercising due diligence on appointed board decisions.
18. Mayor Spencer cited statistics showing a low volume of calls for review, with approximately four to five occurrences per year between 2015 and 2016.
19. The City Council voted 3 to 2 to approve the motion to revise the call for review process.
