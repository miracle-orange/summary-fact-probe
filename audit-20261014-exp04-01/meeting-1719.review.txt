PILOT SMALL FACTS
1. The McKay Avenue Open Space Fiscal Responsibility Measure (Item 60) and the Carrying Alameda Act (Item 66) are being considered for submission to voters.
2. A motion was passed to defer the decision on Item 60 to a special meeting on January 15th, while Item 66, the Carrying Alameda Act, was approved for submission to the ballot with a 4 to 1 vote.

PILOT LARGE FACTS
1. The council is discussing whether to combine items 60 and 66 for the public's convenience.
2. Item 60 involves submitting a resolution titled 'McKay Avenue Open Space Fiscal Responsibility Measure' to voters.
3. Item 6e is a competing resolution titled 'Carrying Alameda Act' proposing voters confirm the city’s prior approval of a specific use.
4. The fiscal responsibility measure (item 6d) requires the city attorney to assess if there is a taking and its financial implications before implementation.
5. Council members are considering whether to direct the city attorney to take legal action without putting the fiscal responsibility measure on the ballot.
6. If the fiscal responsibility measure passes, the city attorney would be authorized to file a lawsuit to determine if there is a taking and calculate its value.
7. Some council members express concern about limiting funding sources to only bond measures due to potential legal and logistical risks.
8. Public speaker Douglas Biggs argues that delaying the project could result in costly penalties outlined in the lease agreement.
9. Jeffrey Riddiford supports the Carrying Alameda Act as it focuses on providing support for people at risk of homelessness.
10. Councilmember Vela prefers not to put the fiscal responsibility measure on the ballot, suggesting the council can give direction directly.
11. Vice Mayor suggests deferring the decision on giving direction to the city attorney until a special meeting on January 15th.
12. A motion is made and passed to continue the discussion on item 6D to January 15th.
13. A motion is proposed and supported to include the Carrying Alameda Act on the ballot, with subcommittee tasked to finalize the language.
14. There is debate among council members regarding the timing of the election—April versus November 2020—with differing views on urgency and feasibility.
15. Councilmembers emphasize the importance of clear communication to voters about the purpose and implications of each measure.
16. One council member opposes the April election timeline for the Carrying Alameda Act, citing lack of compelling evidence for immediate necessity.

REFERENCE FACTS (NOT EXHAUSTIVE GROUND TRUTH)
1. Item 6D is titled the McKay Avenue Open Space Fiscal Responsibility Measure.
2. Item 6E is a resolution to submit an ordinance titled the Carrying Alameda Act to voters.
3. The Carrying Alameda Act proposes that voters confirm the City of Alameda's previous approval of a facility use at the McKay Avenue location.
4. The McKay Avenue Open Space Fiscal Responsibility Measure would compel the City Attorney to file a lawsuit to determine if acquiring the property constitutes a taking and to identify a funding source prior to implementation.
5. Public speaker Doug Biggs stated that delaying the McKay Avenue project beyond two years will incur financial penalties according to the lease.
6. The City Council voted to continue the discussion regarding the Fiscal Responsibility Measure to a special meeting on January 15th.
7. The City Council decided not to place the McKay Avenue Open Space Fiscal Responsibility Measure on the ballot.
8. The City Council intends to direct the City Attorney to file a court action regarding the property value if the citizens' initiative passes, rather than using a ballot measure to mandate this action.
9. The City Council voted 4 to 1 to place the Carrying Alameda Act on the April 9, 2019, special election ballot.
10. Councilmember Daysog voted against placing the Carrying Alameda Act on the April ballot because he prefers a November 2020 election.
11. A subcommittee consisting of the Vice Mayor and Councilmember Oddie was formed to draft the ballot question and arguments for the Carrying Alameda Act.
12. Councilmember Vella and the Mayor are assigned to draft arguments for the measure opposing the Carrying Alameda Act.
